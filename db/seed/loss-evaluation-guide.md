# Vehicle value, unknown party and conflicting fault: evaluation guide

Evaluator reference only. These are fictional facts and provisional evidence-handling expectations, not approved policy terms or final coverage labels. Keep this guide separate from the first-decision agent's inputs.

| Scenario | Facts | Expected reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| damage_exceeds_value | Repair estimate $12,500 exceeds the $8,000 blue book figure on the policy record. | Flag a potential total loss and need for current valuation, salvage and applicable policy rules. Do not equate the blue book figure with a final payment cap or assume that the repair estimate alone proves a total loss. |
| hit_and_run_unknown_other_party | Other party details and plaintiff statement are absent; claimant reports an unknown driver and the police record notes the reported incident. | Identify the missing party and evidence, consider applicable coverages and conditions, and request information as needed. Do not invent a plaintiff's identity or testimony or automatically deny solely because the driver is unknown. |
| opposing_fault_accounts | Claimant and plaintiff both say they had the green light; no independent signal evidence or fault finding is available. | Clearly present both accounts and the unresolved factual conflict. Do not assert who is at fault or assume that a disagreement means either party lied. Refer for review where the missing fact is material. |

The schema does not link a claim to a particular vehicle or policy; matching descriptions are narrative evidence, not proof of a unique link. Approved coverage rules, expert-verified labels and calibration against a larger held-out set remain necessary before scoring confidence as a probability.
