# Police-report contradiction cases: evaluation guide

Evaluator reference only. Fictional examples; these are provisional evidence-handling expectations, not approved coverage outcomes. Do not give this guide to the first-decision agent.

| Scenario | Evidence to compare | What the assessment should recognize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| police_contradicts_claimant | Claimant says they stopped; the police report describes footage showing they did not. The plaintiff says claimant did not stop. | Identify the contradiction and which account the police report tends to support. The report describes footage that is not attached; request the recording and do not assert that the claimant lied or that coverage is necessarily excluded. |
| police_contradicts_plaintiff | Plaintiff says claimant did not stop; the police report describes footage showing claimant stopped and was then struck. | Identify the contradiction and which account the police report tends to support. Request the referenced recording; do not assert the plaintiff lied or that the police report alone establishes coverage. |

The two cases use the same party accounts and vary the report so that reviewers can check for consistent treatment. Police reports can be important evidence but are not automatically conclusive; consider the report's source and its limitations. The current schema does not directly connect a claim to a specific policy or vehicle. An outcome and confidence should reflect these limitations and the absence of approved policy rules.
